# Market of Ash — Political Geography and Regional Map

## Regional design principle

**The Five-Well Basin should feel interdependent rather than divided into good and evil territories.** Every settlement owns, produces, or controls something another settlement needs. Political groups are therefore competing over access to the same network, not simply fighting over abstract ideology.

The player’s caravan is valuable because it can cross boundaries. It moves water from a source to a shortage, scrap from a foundry to a broken bridge, medicine from a licensed store to an exhausted frontier, and information from one route to another. Every delivery can also strengthen a faction, undermine a local group, or make one road more politically important.

The region should be legible through three layers:

| Layer | Player-facing question |
|---|---|
| **Resources** | What does this settlement have, and what does it lack? |
| **Power** | Who can permit, protect, tax, delay, or redirect movement here? |
| **Exposure** | What will break if this route, well, bridge, or relationship fails? |

The design should avoid adding a reputation bar for every group. The two primary axes remain **Warden Control** and **Caravan Openness**. Local groups express themselves through event choices, settlement resilience, contract access, and ending conditions.

## Map overview: The Five-Well Basin

The region is a rough crescent around a dry central basin. Ashgate sits to the northwest, Cinderford lies on the northern slag slopes, Hollow Market occupies the central basin, Brine Cross lies east on the salt flats, and Reedwatch sits southeast at the frontier edge.

```text
                         NORTH
                           ↑
             CINDERFORD ●───────● ASHGATE
              foundry       Toll Road / permits
                  │          ╲
       service    │            ╲ Old Road
       link       │              ╲
                  ● HOLLOW MARKET ───────● BRINE CROSS
                bazaar, rumors       water, salt flats
                       ╲                  │
                        ╲ Dry Cut        │ water / medicine
                         ╲                │
                          ╲         ● REEDWATCH
                           ╲       frontier, grain
                            └───────┘

        WEST: old ash road and ruins   EAST: wells and exposed frontier
```

The diagram is a conceptual layout rather than a fixed navigational graph. The player should see the five canonical settlements and the three first-slice route profiles:

| Route | General connection | Profile | Main value | Main danger |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Old Road** | Ashgate → Hollow Market → Reedwatch | Cheap and exposed | Preserves margin and keeps informal trade moving. | Raiders, false route marks, broken bridges, and vulnerable cargo. |
| **Toll Road** | Ashgate → Cinderford → Brine Cross | Expensive and safer | Maintained surface, official forecasts, and stronger escort coverage. | Permits, sudden tolls, ration inspections, and centralized control. |
| **Dry Cut** | Hollow Market → Brine Cross → Reedwatch | Fast but provision-heavy | Bypasses checkpoints and can reach the frontier before prices change. | Two-day provisioning burden, hidden dry wells, ash wind, and few recovery points. |

A later content layer may add the **Foundry Service Link** between Cinderford and Hollow Market. It should be repair-dependent rather than a free fourth super-route. Its value is moving scrap, charcoal, and repair crews into the basin; its weakness is that worker unrest or bridge damage can close it.

## Political groups

### The Ash Wardens

The Ash Wardens are the region’s official authority. They maintain the Toll Road, issue permits, organize escorts, and keep the ration records. Their public argument is strong: a crisis becomes worse when every merchant invents a private rule for distributing essential goods.

Their problem is that authority has become inseparable from secrecy. Officials fear that a visible failure will prove the Wardens are no longer necessary. As the crisis grows, they may hide falling reserves, alter inspection rules, or use safety as a reason to close routes they cannot actually protect.

The Wardens should offer the player reliable but conditional benefits: safer travel forecasts, access to regulated contracts, reduced exposure on the Toll Road, and priority at official stores. The cost is control. High Warden reputation should bring visible checkpoints, exclusive permits, and a smaller space for independent trade.

The Wardens contain three internal blocs:

| Bloc | Wants | Pressure it creates |
|---|---|---|
| **Warden Officers** | A clear chain of command and predictable movement. | Demand permits and punish public challenges to authority. |
| **Reservoir Auditors** | Accurate stores and an honest account of the shortage. | Ask the player to verify or expose official reports. |
| **Road Escorts** | Stable routes and paid patrol work. | Offer protection but may become a toll-taking interest group. |

The Wardens are not villains. Their roads genuinely are safer. Their strongest flaw is the belief that stability justifies deciding who may move.

### The Free Caravans

The Free Caravans are an informal network of independent traders, route guides, and mutual-aid crews. Their public argument is also strong: goods move when people can move, bargain, and take risks without waiting for a permit.

Their internal contradiction is that open trade benefits unequal players differently. A small caravan may use openness to survive. A large caravan may use it to hoard water, manipulate rumors, or force a weak settlement to accept a bad contract.

The Free Caravans should offer lower informal route costs, more contract variety, shared route rumors, and access to goods that never enter official ledgers. The cost is volatility. High Caravan Openness should keep routes accessible but increase exposure to counterfeit measures, rival escorts, speculative pricing, and uneven distribution.

The Free Caravans contain three internal blocs:

| Bloc | Wants | Pressure it creates |
|---|---|---|
| **Mutual-Aid Caravans** | Open routes and fair access for small traders. | Ask the player to share route information or carry low-margin cargo. |
| **Speculators** | Price freedom and the ability to hold goods until the market peaks. | Offer extraordinary profits during shortages. |
| **Independent Escorts** | Paid protection without Warden oversight. | Reduce travel risk but create uncertainty about who hired them. |

The Free Caravans are not heroes. Their strongest flaw is believing that access alone guarantees fairness.

### The Wellkeepers’ Compact

The Wellkeepers are a local coalition based in Brine Cross. They include well workers, public rationers, and smaller water merchants who want clean water treated as a shared reserve before it becomes a private commodity.

They do not need a permanent reputation axis. Their influence should be represented through settlement resilience and the player’s water decisions. Selling fairly, funding pumps, publishing measures, and protecting well workers should make more recovery choices available. Exploiting scarcity should increase money while making later water events harsher.

Their internal conflict is between public rationers and merchant well owners. Both may believe they are protecting Brine Cross. The difference is whether the reserve remains accessible during the next shortage.

### The Cinder Guild

The Cinder Guild controls much of Cinderford’s scrap processing, charcoal, pump repair, and bridge work. They are a practical industrial coalition rather than a conventional faction. Their public goal is to keep the region physically repairable.

The Guild can supply the materials that make infrastructure possible, but it requires food, water, charcoal, and reliable wages. Its internal conflict is between foundry owners, repair crews, and unpaid workers. A player who delivers scrap but ignores wages may improve a bridge while worsening the labor shortage.

Cinderford should be the place where the player learns that infrastructure has a labor cost. Scrap and charcoal are not abstract upgrade currencies; they are the physical means by which the region remains connected.

### The Reedwatch Frontier League

The Reedwatch Frontier League is a loose alliance of farmers, watchkeepers, militia, and families who refuse to become a line removed from the map. They need grain, water, medicine, and an honest answer about whether the wider region intends to keep the frontier inhabited.

Reedwatch should create the strongest pressure against purely profitable play. It offers cheap grain and useful frontier information, but its needs are expensive to meet. The player can strengthen Reedwatch by delivering grain, carrying medicine, keeping a route open, or supporting controlled evacuation.

The League’s internal conflict is between the militia, farmers, and evacuation advocates. They agree that abandonment is dangerous but disagree about whether the settlement can survive one more shortage.

### The Hollow Measure Brokers

The Hollow Measure Brokers operate inside Hollow Market. They control rumors, informal credit, private storage, and the weighing practices that allow the bazaar to function outside official authority.

Some brokers are honest measurers trying to keep exchange alive when official prices are stale. Others are speculators who profit from confusion. Their content should create uncertainty with a source rather than random noise: the player can verify a measure, buy a rumor, expose a broker, or accept a high-margin risk.

Hollow Market is where Tess Oryn’s social abilities have the greatest value. It should also be the place where the player learns that “free information” is often information that someone wants spread.

## Primary political tensions

### Water: commodity or reserve

This is the central tension. Brine Cross has the strongest water source but is affected by brackish wells and failing pumps. Reedwatch has the highest frontier demand and the weakest reserve. Ashgate has official cisterns and records. Hollow Market can move water privately. Cinderford needs water to keep its repair economy working.

The player should be able to sell at peak price, distribute fairly, keep a caravan reserve, or fund pump repair. These choices should produce different combinations of money, reputation, and settlement resilience.

### Road control and tolls

The Toll Road is safer because someone maintains it. That maintenance gives the Wardens a legitimate reason to charge and inspect. The political problem appears when tolls rise faster than maintenance quality or when permits exclude smaller caravans.

The Old Road is open but exposed. The player can protect the principle of access while accepting higher risk, or use the Toll Road while accepting that the Warden system gains leverage over future movement.

### Who owns the rumor

Information changes prices before goods move. Nara Vey wants useful warnings shared. Tess Oryn knows information can open a door. Hollow Measure Brokers sell uncertainty as a product. The Wardens want reports verified and controlled.

A good event does not ask whether the rumor is true in the abstract. It asks what the player will do before certainty arrives: publish, sell, verify, or keep silent.

### Who gets repaired first

Cinderford can repair a bridge, a pump, a road surface, or an escort vehicle, but not all at once. This makes scrap and charcoal political. A bridge repair may restore trade while leaving a settlement’s water system weak. A pump repair may stabilize Brine Cross while reducing the materials available for the Toll Road.

The player should be able to see this choice on the map through work crews, idle furnaces, broken spans, and delayed contracts.

### The frontier is not a ledger entry

Reedwatch is the test of whether the player sees the region as a set of margins or a living network. The player does not need to save everyone, but the game should make abandonment visible and consequential. A frontier delivery can improve local resilience, preserve future contracts, and create a safer route. Skipping it can create cheaper future travel through a quieter, poorer settlement—or eliminate that opportunity entirely.

## Resource geography

The six core goods have distinct sources, demands, and political owners. Price differences should remain understandable and should shift as the crisis changes.

| Good | Main source or abundance | Main demand | Political owner or influence | Gameplay purpose |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Water** | Brine Cross, Ashgate cisterns, hidden springs. | Reedwatch, Brine Cross, Ashgate. | Wellkeepers and Wardens. | Central crisis good; high profit versus resilience tension. |
| **Grain** | Reedwatch fields and Ashgate stores. | Cinderford, Brine Cross, Ashgate. | Reedwatch League and Wardens. | Makes frontier support and industrial stability interdependent. |
| **Scrap** | Cinderford salvage yards and Old Road ruins. | Cinderford, Ashgate, Reedwatch. | Cinder Guild and Free Caravans. | Repairs bridges, pumps, and caravan infrastructure. |
| **Medicine** | Ashgate licensed stores and Hollow Market reserves. | Reedwatch, Brine Cross, Cinderford. | Wardens and Hollow Brokers. | Turns water shortage and travel risk into human consequences. |
| **Charcoal** | Cinderford furnaces and woodland burn sites. | Cinderford, Ashgate, Brine Cross. | Cinder Guild and Wardens. | Keeps repair and production possible without replacing trade. |
| **Dyed cloth** | Hollow Market weavers and Reedwatch reed fiber. | Ashgate, Cinderford, Reedwatch. | Free Caravans and Hollow Brokers. | Flexible margin good when water trade is politically costly. |

The most important dependency chain is:

> **Reedwatch grain → Cinderford workers → pump and bridge repairs → Brine Cross water → Ashgate and frontier access.**

The player should not see this as a single quest chain. They should discover it through prices, event reports, settlement visuals, and the consequences of choosing one delivery over another.

## Obstacles

Obstacles should be forecastable, visible, and recoverable. They are most interesting when they change the value of a route rather than simply applying a random penalty.

| Obstacle | Visible warning | What it threatens | Player responses |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ash wind** | Faded route markers and reduced horizon visibility. | Information quality and travel-risk forecasts. | Scout, delay, take the Toll Road, or follow a local guide. |
| **Dry well** | Crossed-out well markers and empty jars. | Water, provisions, and crisis stage. | Carry reserve, buy early, fund pump repair, or reroute. |
| **Broken bridge** | Dropped span and stranded repair crew. | Heavy cargo, time, and route continuity. | Supply scrap, pay a ferry, abandon heavy goods, or take Dry Cut. |
| **Warden checkpoint** | Lantern queue, chalk marks, and inspection boards. | Money, time, and political access. | Pay, negotiate, show a ledger, or take Old Road. |
| **Raider shadow** | Missing markers, distant watchfires, and uncertain reports. | Cargo safety and escort cost. | Hire escort, travel light, share warning, or choose a safer route. |
| **Counterfeit measures** | Price card conflicts with local weights. | Trade value and rumor trust. | Verify, use Tess, accept risk, or report the broker. |
| **Refugee flow** | Crowded settlement marker and changed demand report. | Provisions, labor, and settlement resilience. | Carry grain, medicine, escort evacuation, or prioritize a contract. |

The game should distinguish between a cost that the player knowingly accepted and a surprise that the game failed to communicate. A route event may be uncertain, but the uncertainty must have a named source.

## Crisis map progression

The water shortage should visibly alter the map over four stages.

| Stage | Map state | Political change | New player question |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0 — Routine Traffic** | Normal market icons, open route labels, ordinary checkpoints. | Factions compete for future loyalty. | Which route and cargo create the best next opportunity? |
| **1 — Thin Wells** | Smaller water icons, higher medicine demand, ration marks at Ashgate, empty jars at Brine Cross. | Wardens request records; Wellkeepers request fair distribution; Free Caravans request open movement. | Do I treat the shortage as a price signal or a public emergency? |
| **2 — Managed Shortage** | Checkpoint queues, expensive Dry Cut guides, thin Reedwatch watchfires, delayed pump repairs at Cinderford. | No faction can satisfy every settlement through ordinary trade. | Which relationship or route should carry the next sacrifice? |
| **3 — The Basin Decides** | Restricted routes, dominant water contracts, final infrastructure offer, visible settlement outcome markers. | The player’s political posture is now readable before the ending choice. | What kind of region should exist after the crisis? |

## Design guardrails

The map must remain readable at normal zoom. A player should be able to identify a settlement by silhouette, banner, and resource signal without opening a lore menu.

Every major resource should have at least one source, one destination, one political influence, and one recovery route. If water is scarce, the player should be able to respond through reserve, rerouting, repair, fair distribution, or political access—not only by paying the highest price.

Obstacles should cost money, time, cargo, trust, or access, but should not silently delete the campaign. A failed route should create a new map problem that can be understood and addressed.

Local groups should create texture and event choices without adding a separate reputation meter for every organization. The two major faction axes, settlement resilience, crew relationships, and visible map changes are enough to express the political geography.

Production should remain out of scope for the initial slice. Cinderford may repair pumps and bridges, but the player should not build factories that eliminate the need to trade. The political economy works only if goods continue to move through vulnerable routes.

## Implementation guidance

The stable IDs and structured content live in `content/political_geography.json`. The first implementation slice should render the five settlements, route profiles, two primary reputation axes, and one visible resource-flow overlay. The second should connect one obstacle to route preview and event availability. The third should implement crisis-stage map changes and a settlement resilience value.

Future agents should treat this document as the content contract. They should not add a faction, resource, or route unless it changes a player decision and can be represented through a visible map signal, a deterministic state change, and a recoverable outcome.
