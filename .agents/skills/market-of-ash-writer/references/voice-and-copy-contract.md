# Voice and copy contract

## Voice

Market of Ash speaks like a practiced caravan ledger kept by people who live with scarcity. It is observant, material, restrained, and slightly wary. It does not narrate the player as a hero.

- Use short declarative sentences for interface guidance.
- Use one concrete sensory detail to distinguish a place or road.
- Name who needs a good and why when that fact supports trade.
- Describe factions through what they regulate, protect, charge, reveal, or withhold.
- Let characters express expertise through the option they create and the limit attached to it.
- End consequences with the changed fact or viable next move.

## Information order

For commitment text:

1. Action and destination.
2. Exact cost or resource.
3. Exposed asset and uncertainty.
4. Likely consequence.
5. Recovery, when relevant.

For result text:

1. What happened.
2. What changed numerically or structurally.
3. Why it happened.
4. What remains possible.

## Regional vocabulary

Use these as boundaries, not mandatory word lists.

| Region | Material language | Social pressure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Five-Well Basin | cisterns, seals, span iron, reed grain, ration lines, old milestones | Warden permits versus shared roads |
| Glasswind Reach | saltglass, kiln heat, mirrored lamps, shardwind, shelter cairns | licensed beacons versus unregistered signals |
| Siltfire March | peat shelves, resin lamps, reed mats, bell lines, smoke marks | official wards versus local pathfinders |

## Surface limits

- Button: verb plus object; add cost only when it changes commitment.
- Heading: one idea, normally under eight words.
- Tutorial body: two short sentences; instruction last.
- Market or route description: one economic fact plus one physical identity.
- Event setup: one immediate obstruction or offer, not backstory.
- Choice: action first, then exact cost and consequence.
- Receipt: result first, delta second, next implication last.

These are editing defaults, not character-count gates. Preserve necessary meaning and accessibility over arbitrary brevity.

## Review questions

1. Can the player identify the next decision without reading the line twice?
2. Does each important number have a named cause?
3. Is uncertainty distinguished from a guaranteed result?
4. Does the wording preserve a viable refusal or recovery path?
5. Could the same line appear in another settlement unchanged? If so, add one material local detail or remove decorative prose.
